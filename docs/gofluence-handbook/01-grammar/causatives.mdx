# Causatives

## 1. Learning objective

Use the causative verbs **have, get, make and let** to say who causes an action, and how strong the force is.

## 2. Key concepts

A causative verb says that one person or thing **causes another person or thing to do something**. Each verb has a different degree of force.

| Verb | Force | Structure | Example |
|------|-------|-----------|---------|
| make | strong (no choice) | make + person + base verb | The manager made us redo the report. |
| have | neutral (a request or an assigned task) | have + person + base verb | I had the intern book the room. |
| get | needs a little persuasion | get + person + **to** + base verb | I got my colleague to review my code. |
| let | gives permission | let + person + base verb | My boss let me work from home. |

### The examples from the notes

- The policeman **made** him stop the car.
- After checking all my documents, the officer **let** me enter the building.

### Causative with things (passive form)

When the result is done by someone else, use **have / get + object + past participle**.

- I had my laptop repaired. (someone repaired it)
- She got the contract signed. (someone signed it)

:::tip Remember the pattern
- `make`, `have`, `let` + person + **base verb** (no `to`)
- `get` + person + **to** + base verb
- `have / get` + thing + **past participle**
:::

:::note Strength
From strongest to softest: `make` (forced), `have` (arranged), `get` (convinced), `let` (allowed).
:::

:::warning Make in the passive
In the passive, `make` needs `to`: He **was made to stop** the car.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| My boss made me to work late. | My boss made me work late. | No `to` after `make` in the active voice. |
| I got my colleague review the code. | I got my colleague **to review** the code. | `get` needs `to`. |
| She let me to leave early. | She let me leave early. | No `to` after `let`. |
| I repaired my laptop. (a technician did it) | I **had** my laptop **repaired**. | Use `have + object + past participle` when someone else does it. |
| The manager had us to submit the form. | The manager had us submit the form. | No `to` after `have`. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
Spanish uses *hacer* for most causatives (*me hizo trabajar*, *hice reparar el portátil*). English picks different verbs: `make` is forced, `let` is permission. Also, English does not use `to` after `make`, `have` and `let`.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. The manager ______ (make) us ______ (stay) late. It was not our choice.
2. Our company ______ (let) employees ______ (choose) their own laptops.
3. I ______ (get) the client ______ (sign) the contract after two calls.
4. We ______ (have) the office ______ (clean) every Friday. (someone cleans it)

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Use the causative verb in brackets.

1. A technician fixed my phone. (have)
2. My manager allowed me to attend the conference. (let)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Tell me about something your boss **made** you do and something your boss **let** you do.
- What did you **have** someone do for you recently?

## 8. Quick recap

- Causatives show one person or thing causing an action.
- Force: `make` (strong), `have`, `get`, `let` (permission).
- `make / have / let` + person + base verb. `get` + person + `to` + base verb.
- `have / get` + thing + past participle when someone else does the job.
