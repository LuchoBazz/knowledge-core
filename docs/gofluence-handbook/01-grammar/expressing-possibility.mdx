# Expressing possibility

## 1. Learning objective

Say that something is possible using **modals + base verb** and **adverbs of uncertainty**.

## 2. Key concepts

Use a modal with the **base form** of the verb to say that something is possible. You are not sure. You only say it can happen.

| Modal | Structure | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| can / could | subject + can/could + base verb | This protocol can decrease hospital infections a lot. |
| may / might | subject + may/might + base verb | They may agree with our proposition. |

The modals are the same for all subjects: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.

:::tip Which modal is stronger?
For possibility, `can`, `could`, `may` and `might` are close in meaning. `Might` and `may` often show more doubt. `Could` is very common and safe in meetings.
:::

### Adverbs of uncertainty

You can also use adverbs. They show that you are not 100% sure.

| Adverb | Position | Example |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Maybe | start of the sentence | Maybe they'll like our new hygiene protocol. |
| Perhaps | start of the sentence | Perhaps they'll like our new protocol. |
| Possibly | between `will` and the verb | They'll possibly ask for statistics about our research. |

:::note `maybe` vs `may be`
`Maybe` (one word) is an adverb: *Maybe we can finish today.* `May be` (two words) is a modal + verb: *The client may be late.*
:::

:::warning One modal per verb
*Maybe we could…* is fine and polite. But never put two modals together (*we might could*). Keep one modal per verb. See [general modal rules](./general-modal-rules.mdx).
:::

:::danger Spanish speakers
Do not use `to` after `may` or `might`. Say *We might go*, not *We might to go*. Also, English does not need `that` after `maybe` (*quizás que*): say *Maybe we can finish*, not *Maybe that we can finish*.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| They mights agree. | They **might** agree. | Modals never take `-s`. |
| They may to agree. | They may **agree**. | No `to` after a modal. |
| They may agrees with us. | They may **agree** with us. | Base form after a modal. |
| May be we can finish today. | **Maybe** we can finish today. | Use the adverb `maybe` at the start. |
| They possibly will ask for data. | They will **possibly** ask for data. | `Possibly` goes after `will`. |

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. The new protocol ______ (can / decrease) infections a lot.
2. They ______ (might / agree) with our proposition.
3. ______ (Maybe / May be) they will like our report.
4. They will ______ (possibly) ask for statistics.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence to express possibility. Use the word in brackets.

1. It is possible that the client will say no. (might)
2. It is possible that we will change the deadline. (could)
3. Perhaps the team likes the plan. (will possibly)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Talk about **three possible results** of a new project using `could`, `may` and `might`.
- What will your manager possibly ask in the next meeting?

## 8. Quick recap

- Possibility = modal + base verb: can, could, may, might.
- Modals do not change form and take no `to`.
- Adverbs: `maybe` and `perhaps` go first, `possibly` goes after `will`.
- `Maybe` is one word. `May be` is a modal and a verb.
