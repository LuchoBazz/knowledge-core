# General modal rules

## 1. Learning objective

Use modal verbs (`can`, `must`, `will`, `should`, `might`) correctly in the present, future and past.

## 2. Key concepts

Modals are helper verbs. They add meaning such as ability, obligation, advice or possibility. They all follow the same five rules.

| # | Rule | Example |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | Modals do not change form for singular or plural subjects. | She **can** help. They **can** help. |
| 2 | The verb after a modal is always in the base form. | We should **send** the report. |
| 3 | A verb is attached to only one modal. | We **will be able to** finish. |
| 4 | Present and future: modal + base verb. | I **can join** the call now. |
| 5 | Past: modal + `have` + past participle. | He **should have called** the client. |

:::tip Quick formula
- Present or future: `modal + base verb`
- Past: `modal + have + past participle`
:::

### Rule 1: no `-s`, no change

Normal verbs add `-s` in the third person (*she works*). Modals never do.

- Correct: She **can** speak English.
- Wrong: She cans speak English.

### Rule 2: base form only

The base form is the verb without `to`, `-s`, `-ed` or `-ing`.

- Correct: He must **finish** today.
- Wrong: He must finishes / to finish / finishing today.

### Rule 3: only one modal

You cannot put two modals together. Use a substitute expression for the second one.

- Wrong: We will can deliver on time.
- Correct: We will **be able to** deliver on time.

### Rules 4 and 5: present, future and past

To talk about the past, use `have` + past participle after the modal.

| Modal | Past form | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| should | should have + past participle | You should have told me earlier. |
| must | must have + past participle | The server must have crashed. |
| might | might have + past participle | The email might have gone to spam. |
| can't | can't have + past participle | She can't have forgotten the meeting. |

:::note `can` in the past
For ability in the past, use `could` (*I could code at 12*), not `can have`.
:::

:::warning Do not use `to` after a modal
Do not say *I must to go* or *She should to call*. Use the base verb directly.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| She cans help you. | She **can** help you. | Modals never take `-s`. |
| We should to send the report. | We should **send** the report. | No `to` after a modal. |
| He must finishes today. | He must **finish** today. | Use the base form. |
| We will can deliver on Monday. | We will **be able to** deliver on Monday. | Only one modal per verb. |
| You should told me. | You should **have told** me. | Past: modal + have + past participle. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
In Spanish, the second verb is an infinitive (*puedo ir*, *debo ir*, *tengo que ir*), so many learners add `to` in English too. After a modal there is no `to`: `can go`, `must go`, `should go`. Only `have to` and `be able to` include `to`.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

Choose the correct form of the verb in brackets.

1. She can ______ (speak) three languages.
2. We should ______ (check) the numbers again.
3. They might ______ (be) late for the meeting.
4. You must ______ (show) your ID at the entrance.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Fix each sentence or change it to the past.

1. He musts send the report today.
2. We will can finish on time.
3. I should call the client. (past: I did not call)
4. The server must crash last night. (the speaker is sure)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Give **three pieces of advice** to a new colleague using `should`.
- Talk about a mistake in a past project using `should have`.

## 8. Quick recap

- Modals do not change for singular or plural subjects.
- After a modal, always use the base form of the verb.
- One verb, one modal. Use `be able to` as a substitute.
- Present and future: modal + base verb.
- Past: modal + have + past participle.
