# Making comparisons

:::info Assumptions
The class notes cover simple comparisons with comparative adjectives (`-er than`, `more ... than`, `as ... as`). This page adds `less ... than`, `not as ... as` and a short note on superlatives to make the topic complete.
:::

## 1. Learning objective

Compare two people, tools or options using **comparative adjectives** and **as + adjective + as**.

## 2. Key concepts

Use a comparative adjective to show a difference between two things. Use `as ... as` to show they are equal.

| Type | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Short adjective | adjective + `-er` + than | Frances is friendlier than Alice. |
| Long adjective | more + adjective + than | Alice is more experienced than Frances. |
| Equal | as + adjective + as | Frances is as motivated as the other nurses in the clinic. |
| Less | less + adjective + than | This plan is less expensive than the old one. |
| Not equal | not as + adjective + as | The old tool is not as fast as the new one. |

:::tip How to choose `-er` or `more`
- One syllable: add `-er` (fast, faster; cheap, cheaper).
- Two syllables ending in `-y`: change `y` to `i` and add `-er` (easy, easier; busy, busier).
- Two or more syllables: use `more` (experienced, more experienced; reliable, more reliable).
:::

:::note Irregular adjectives
`good` becomes `better`. `bad` becomes `worse`. `far` becomes `further` or `farther`.
:::

### Spelling rules

- Short adjective ending in one vowel + one consonant: double the consonant (big, bigger; hot, hotter).
- Adjective ending in `-e`: add `-r` (large, larger; safe, safer).

:::warning Use `than`, not `that`
Comparatives use **than**. With `as ... as`, do not use `than`.
- Correct: Our tool is faster **than** theirs.
- Correct: Our tool is as fast **as** theirs.
:::

### Superlatives (one extra note)

To compare three or more things, use **the + adjective + -est** or **the most + adjective**. Example: She is the most experienced developer on the team.

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Alice is more experienced that Frances. | Alice is more experienced **than** Frances. | Use `than` after a comparative. |
| Frances is more friendlier than Alice. | Frances is friendlier than Alice. | Do not use `more` and `-er` together. |
| This tool is more good than that one. | This tool is **better** than that one. | `good` is irregular: better. |
| She is as motivated than the others. | She is as motivated **as** the others. | Use `as ... as` for equal. |
| The server is more fast than the old one. | The server is **faster** than the old one. | Short adjectives take `-er`. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
In Spanish we say *más ... que* and *tan ... como*. In English, the second word is **than** for difference and **as** for equality. Also, do not translate *más bueno*: use **better**.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. Our new laptop is ______ (fast) than the old one.
2. The manager is ______ (experienced) than the new intern.
3. This report is ______ (good) than the last one.
4. Sara is as ______ (motivated) ______ her teammates.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence with the word in brackets.

1. Vendor A costs 500 dollars. Vendor B costs 700 dollars. (cheaper)
2. The old process is slow. The new process is slow too. (as ... as)
3. Tool B is very flexible. Tool A is not very flexible. (less)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Compare **two tools** you use at work (for example Slack and email).
- Compare your job today with your job two years ago.

## 8. Quick recap

- Short adjectives: `-er than` (faster than).
- Long adjectives: `more ... than` (more experienced than).
- Equal: `as ... as` (as motivated as).
- Irregular: good, better; bad, worse.
- Never use `more` and `-er` together.

## 9. Review checklist

- [ ] I can compare two options using `-er than` and `more ... than`.
- [ ] I can use `as ... as` to say two things are equal.
- [ ] I know the irregular forms better and worse.
- [ ] I use `than` and `as` correctly.
- [ ] I can compare two tools aloud in a meeting.
