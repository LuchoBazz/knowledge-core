# Making predictions

## 1. Learning objective

Predict future events at work and show how sure you are, using **will**, adverbs like `definitely` and `probably`, and the modals **may / might / could**.

## 2. Key concepts

A prediction is what you think will happen. Your words show how **certain** you are.

```text title="Certainty scale"
100%   will surely / will definitely + base verb
 80%   will probably + base verb
 50%   may / might / could + base verb
  0%   will not / won't + base verb
```

### Express certainty or uncertainty

- He will surely finish the report today.
- She will definitely join the call.
- It will not work without the update.
- The client won't definitely agree. (not sure they will agree)

:::warning Word order with adverbs
With `will`, the adverb goes **after** `will` in positive sentences: `will definitely`, `will probably`.
In negative sentences, it goes **before** `won't`: `definitely won't`, `probably won't`.
:::

### Express possibility

- I think he can finish it by Friday.
- The release may slip to next week.
- The team might need more time.
- It could be a good opportunity.
- She will probably send the budget tomorrow.

:::tip Modals use the base verb
After `will`, `would`, `may`, `might`, `can` and `could`, use the **base verb** without `to`.
It might rain. (not: It might to rain.)
:::

### Modal verbs for predictions

| Modals | Use | Example |
|--------|-----|---------|
| will / would | strong prediction (`would` is more polite or hypothetical) | The new tool will save us time. |
| may / might | possible, about 50% | We might miss the deadline. |
| can / could | possible, a general ability or chance | The server could crash under heavy load. |

:::note May, might and could
`may`, `might` and `could` are very close in meaning. `might` and `could` sound a little less sure than `may`.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| It might to rain tomorrow. | It might rain tomorrow. | No `to` after a modal. |
| She will definitely to join. | She will definitely join. | Base verb after `will`. |
| He wills probably finish. | He will probably finish. | `will` never takes `-s`. |
| It will maybe rain. | It will **probably** rain. / It **may** rain. | `maybe` does not go after `will`. Use `probably`, or use `may` or `might`. |
| I am thinking it will work. | I think it will work. | `think` for an opinion uses the simple form. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
In Spanish you can predict with the present (*Mañana lo termino*) or with the future ending (*terminará*). In English, use a modal: `will`, `may`, `might`. Also, `might` is not a past tense here. It talks about the **future**.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

Use `will`, `will not`, `might` or `could`, and the verb in brackets.

1. I am 100% sure. The update ______ (fix) the bug.
2. I am not sure. The client ______ (ask) for changes.
3. The traffic is terrible. We ______ (arrive) late.
4. I am certain it is impossible. The team ______ (finish) by tomorrow.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence with the adverb in brackets.

1. The budget will be approved. (probably)
2. The server will fail during the demo. (definitely not, use `won't`)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Make **three predictions** about your next project. Use one sure, one probable and one possible.
- What will your team probably do next quarter?

## 8. Quick recap

- Show certainty with `will surely`, `will definitely` and `will not`.
- Show possibility with `may`, `might`, `could` and `will probably`.
- Use the base verb after every modal.
- Put the adverb after `will` and before `won't`.

## 9. Review checklist

- [ ] I can order phrases from most sure to least sure.
- [ ] I know when to use `will probably` and when to use `might`.
- [ ] I use the base verb without `to` after modals.
- [ ] I place `definitely` and `probably` correctly.
- [ ] I can make three predictions about my work aloud.
