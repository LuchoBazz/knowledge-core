# Must

## 1. Learning objective

Use `must` to express **obligation** and to give a **confident guess** about something you cannot see directly.

## 2. Key concepts

`Must` is a modal verb. It has two main uses. Follow the [general modal rules](./general-modal-rules.mdx): no `-s`, and always a base verb after it.

### Use 1: obligation

Use `must` for a strong rule or duty.

- You must show an ID.
- He must be paid when the job is done.

```text title="Structure"
subject + must + base verb
```

### Use 2: confident guess

Use `must` when you are almost sure something is true, because you have evidence.

- Wendy's absent; she must be sick.
- The restaurant is always full; it must have good food.

| Time | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Present | must + base verb | She must be tired. |
| Past | must have + past participle | She must have worked late. |

:::tip Obligation or guess?
Look at the context. A rule or order means **obligation**. A situation with evidence means **guess**.
- You must wear a badge. (rule)
- You must be tired. (guess, you look tired)
:::

:::warning `must not` and `don't have to` are different
- `You mustn't share the password.` = it is **forbidden**.
- `You don't have to attend.` = it is **not necessary**, but you can.
:::

:::note Past obligation
`Must` has no past form for obligation. Use `had to`: *We had to submit the report yesterday.*
:::

:::danger Spanish speakers
Spanish *deber* can mean both obligation and guess (*debe de estar enfermo*). In English, do not use `must` for a guess about the future. For the future, use `will` or `may`. For a guess about the present or past, use `must be` or `must have`.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| He musts show an ID. | He **must** show an ID. | Modals never take `-s`. |
| You must to wear a badge. | You must **wear** a badge. | No `to` after `must`. |
| She must sick. | She must **be** sick. | Use `be` before an adjective. |
| He must forgot the meeting. | He must **have forgotten** the meeting. | Past guess: must have + past participle. |
| You must not attend. (when it is optional) | You **don't have to** attend. | `mustn't` means forbidden, not unnecessary. |

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. All employees ______ (must / complete) the training by Friday.
2. Nobody answers the phone. They ______ (must / be) at lunch.
3. The report is wrong. Someone ______ (must / change) the data.
4. You ______ (must / not / share) client data.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence with `must`.

1. I am sure that Ana is tired after the trip.
2. It is necessary for you to show your ID.
3. I am sure that the client read the email yesterday.

### C. Speaking prompts

- Say **three rules** in your office using `must` and `mustn't`.
- Your colleague is absent. Make **two guesses** about why using `must be` and `must have`.

## 8. Quick recap

- `Must` = obligation (a strong rule) or a confident guess.
- Obligation: must + base verb.
- Guess about the present: must be. Guess about the past: must have + past participle.
- `Mustn't` = forbidden. `Don't have to` = not necessary.
- Past obligation uses `had to`.
