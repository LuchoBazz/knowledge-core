# Present perfect vs. simple past

## 1. Learning objective

Decide when to use the **present perfect** (`have / has + past participle`) and when to use the **simple past** to talk about the past at work.

## 2. Key concepts

Both tenses talk about the past. The difference is the **connection to now**.

```text title="Structure"
Present perfect:  have / has + past participle   (I have finished.)
Simple past:      verb + -ed / irregular form    (I finished.)
```

### Present perfect

Use the present perfect when:

1. The subject **has a past experience**: I have managed a remote team.
2. An action started at an unspecified or unknown time and **is not finished**, or time is not important: She has worked here for three years.
3. An action is finished but **still affects the present**: The server has crashed, so nobody can log in.
4. In the negative, you talk about something you **expected but did not happen**: The client hasn't replied yet.

### Simple past

Use the simple past when:

1. The subject **did an action in the past**: I sent the report.
2. The action **began and ended** in the past: We worked late last night.
3. The action is **not connected to the present**: The team launched the product in 2022.
4. In the negative, it says an action **did not happen**: He didn't attend the meeting.

| | Present perfect | Simple past |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Not specific, or still open | Finished time (yesterday, last week, in 2022) |
| Link to now | Yes | No |
| Example | I have updated the ticket. | I updated the ticket yesterday. |

:::tip Time words
- Present perfect: `ever, never, already, yet, just, for, since, so far, this week`
- Simple past: `yesterday, last week, ago, in 2022, when, at 3 pm`
:::

:::warning Do not mix a finished time with the present perfect
You cannot say a finished time with `have`. Say **I sent** the email yesterday, not I have sent the email yesterday.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| I have sent the report yesterday. | I **sent** the report yesterday. | Finished time word needs the simple past. |
| I worked here for five years. (still works) | I **have worked** here for five years. | The action continues now. |
| Did you ever lead a team? | **Have you ever** led a team? | `ever` asks about life experience. |
| She has went to the meeting. | She has **gone** to the meeting. | Use the past participle after `have / has`. |
| When have you joined the company? | When **did** you join the company? | `When` asks for a finished time. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
Spanish often uses the simple form for both (*Trabajé aquí cinco años* and *He trabajado aquí cinco años*). In English the choice changes the meaning. Ask yourself: **is it connected to now?** If yes, present perfect. If it is finished, simple past.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

Use the present perfect or the simple past.

1. We ______ (launch) the website in March last year.
2. I ______ (not finish) the slides yet.
3. She ______ (work) at this company since 2019.
4. ______ you ever ______ (present) at a conference?

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Correct each sentence.

1. I have talked to the client yesterday.
2. He worked in this team for six years and he is still here.

### C. Speaking prompts

- Tell your partner **three things you have done** in your career.
- Describe one task you finished **last week**. Use the simple past.

## 8. Quick recap

- Present perfect = past with a link to now (experience, unfinished or recent results).
- Simple past = finished action, usually with a time.
- Time words help: `for, since, yet, already, ever` vs `yesterday, ago, last week`.
- Negative: `haven't done yet` = expected, not yet. `didn't do` = it did not happen.
