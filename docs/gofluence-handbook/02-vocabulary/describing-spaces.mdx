# Describing spaces

## 1. Learning objective

Describe a room, an office or a workstation using four tools: `this is / these are`, `there is / there are`, prepositions of place and adjectives.

## 2. Key concepts

Use four simple steps to describe any space.

**Step 1. Say whose space it is: `this is` / `these are` + possessive.**

```text title="This is / these are"
This is my room.
These are the workers' cubicles.
```

**Step 2. Say what is in the space: `there is` / `there are`.**

```text title="There is / there are"
There is a desk in his office.
There are books in her workstation.
```

**Step 3. Say where things are: prepositions of place.**

```text title="Prepositions of place"
There is a clock on the wall beside his desk.
There are pieces of paper under the table in the corner.
```

**Step 4. Say what the space is like: adjectives.**

| Adjective pair | Example |
|----------------|---------|
| big / small | My room is big. |
| spacious / cramped | His cubicle is spacious. |
| clean / messy | Her workstation is clean. |
| well-lit / dim | The office is well-lit. |

:::tip Singular and plural
`This` and `there is` are for **one** thing. `These` and `there are` are for **more than one** thing.
:::

:::note Possessives
Use possessives with `this is` / `these are`: my, your, his, her, our, their. For one person, add `'s` (Ana's desk). For many people, the apostrophe goes after the `s` (the workers' cubicles).
:::

:::warning `Spacious` vs `cramped`
`Spacious` is positive (a lot of space). `Cramped` is negative (too little space). Do not use them as synonyms.
:::

:::danger Spanish speakers
In Spanish we say *hay* for singular and plural. In English you must choose: `there is` (one) or `there are` (many). Also, do not start with *Is a desk in my office*. Always say `There is a desk in my office`.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| There are a desk in his office. | There **is** a desk in his office. | One thing, so use `is`. |
| There is books in her workstation. | There **are** books in her workstation. | Plural noun, so use `are`. |
| This are the workers cubicles. | **These are** the **workers'** cubicles. | `These` for plural, and a possessive apostrophe. |
| His cubicle is **spacy**. | His cubicle is **spacious**. | The adjective is `spacious`. |
| There is a clock **in** the wall. | There is a clock **on** the wall. | Use `on` for a surface. |

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. ______ is my desk. (this / these)
2. ______ are the team's computers. (this / these)
3. There ______ a printer in the corner. (is / are)
4. There ______ two monitors on the desk. (is / are)
5. The room is dark. It is ______. (well-lit / dim)

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence using the structure in brackets.

1. A lamp is next to my laptop. (There is ... )
2. My cubicle is too small. (Use cramped.)
3. The desk of the workers is clean. (Use these are + workers')

### C. Speaking prompts

- Describe your **workstation** in five sentences. Use at least two prepositions.
- Compare two spaces you know (for example, your office and your home). Use adjectives.
- Describe a **messy** room and then a **clean** one.

## 8. Quick recap

- `This is` / `these are` + possessive introduces a space: This is my room.
- `There is` + one thing. `There are` + more than one thing.
- Use prepositions like `on`, `beside` and `under` to say where things are.
- Use adjectives to describe the space: big, small, spacious, cramped, clean, messy, well-lit, dim.
- Plural possessive: the workers' cubicles.
