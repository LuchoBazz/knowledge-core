# Most commonly used prefixes

## 1. Learning objective

Understand the meaning of ten common prefixes and use them to build and guess new words in workplace English.

## 2. Key concepts

A **prefix** goes at the **beginning** of a base word. It changes the meaning and creates a new word. The base word does not change.

```text title="Structure"
prefix + base word = new word
un-   + happy      = unhappy
re-   + write      = rewrite
```

| Prefix | Meaning | Example from the lesson | Spanish hint |
|--------|---------|-------------------------|--------------|
| `anti-` | against | antibiotics | anti-, contra |
| `pro-` | in favor of, supporting | pro-war | pro-, a favor de |
| `pre-` | before | preschool | pre-, antes de |
| `post-` | after | postgraduate | post-, después de |
| `ex-` | former, previous | ex-president | ex-, antiguo |
| `in-` | not, the opposite of | informal | in-, no |
| `im-` | not, the opposite of | impossible | im-, no |
| `non-` | not, without | nonsmoking | no, sin |
| `un-` | not, the opposite of | unhappy | no, des- |
| `re-` | again | rewrite | re-, otra vez |

:::tip Four prefixes, one idea: "not"
`in-`, `im-`, `non-` and `un-` all make the **opposite** meaning. Learn each one with the words you hear most often. There is no easy rule for which one to use.
:::

:::note `im-` comes before b, m and p
`im-` is a spelling form of `in-`. We usually use `im-` before the letters **b, m, p**: impossible, impolite, immature, imbalance.
:::

:::warning Hyphens
Use a hyphen with `ex-` (ex-manager) and often with `pro-` or `anti-` when the word is new or unusual. Most other prefixes join the word directly: preschool, unhappy, rewrite. When you are not sure, check a dictionary.
:::

:::danger Spanish speakers
Do not translate Spanish prefixes word by word. In English, `un-` is very common (unclear, unavailable), but Spanish uses `des-`, `in-` or `no` in similar cases. Also, `ex-` means **former**, not **external**: an ex-colleague is a person who used to be your colleague.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| The task is **inpossible**. | The task is **impossible**. | Use `im-` before the letter p. |
| He is an **unformal** person. | He is an **informal** person. | Informal takes `in-`, not `un-`. |
| We will hold a **pre-launch** review after the launch. | We will hold a **post-launch** review after the launch. | `pre-` means before, `post-` means after. |
| She is my **ex** manager. | She is my **ex-manager**. | Join `ex-` to the noun with a hyphen. |
| Please **again write** the report. | Please **rewrite** the report. | `re-` already means "again". |

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

Add the correct prefix: `un-`, `im-`, `re-`, `pre-` or `non-`.

1. We need to ______ send the invoice because it has a mistake.
2. It is ______ possible to open the file without a password.
3. The room is a ______ smoking area.
4. The ______ meeting starts at 9:00, before the main session.
5. The customer is ______ happy with the service.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence with a prefix instead of the words in bold.

1. The manager is **not happy** with the results.
2. We have to **write the report again**.
3. She is the **former** CEO of the company. (use ex-)
4. The project is **not possible** with this budget.

### C. Speaking prompts

- Describe your job using **four words** with prefixes.
- Talk about a time something was **unclear** or **impossible** at work. What did you do?
- Name one thing you often **redo** or **rewrite** in your work.

## 8. Quick recap

- A prefix goes at the **start** of a word and changes its meaning.
- `anti-` = against, `pro-` = in favor of, `pre-` = before, `post-` = after.
- `ex-` = former, `re-` = again.
- `in-`, `im-`, `non-` and `un-` all mean **not**. Use `im-` before b, m and p.
- The base word does not change when you add a prefix.
