# Office rooms, furniture and appliances

## 1. Learning objective

Describe an office by naming its rooms, furniture and equipment, and give simple directions to a visitor or a new colleague.

## 2. Key concepts

Here are the main places in an office and what you find in them.

| Place | What you find there |
|-------|---------------------|
| Reception area | front desk, time clock |
| Cubicle | desk, chair, computer |
| Conference room | whiteboard, LCD projector |
| Cafeteria | food and tables (people buy food and eat) |
| Washroom | sinks and toilets (people wash their hands and use the toilet) |

```text title="Useful sentences"
The front desk and time clock are in the reception area.
Each cubicle has a desk, a chair, and a computer.
A conference room usually has a whiteboard and an LCD projector.
```

:::note The word "office" has two meanings
`Office` can mean the **whole place** where a company is (*I work in a big office*). It can also mean a **room** that is bigger than a cubicle (*The manager has her own office*).
:::

:::tip Use `have` and `there is` for contents
- Each cubicle **has** a desk. (the place is the subject)
- **There is** a whiteboard in the conference room. (the thing is the focus)
:::

:::warning Singular or plural?
`The front desk and time clock are...` has two things, so use `are`. `The cafeteria is...` has one place, so use `is`.
:::

:::danger Spanish speakers
`Washroom` is common in Canadian English and in formal places. In other countries you may also hear `restroom` or `bathroom`. Do not use `toilet` in a formal office conversation. Also remember that `office` is not only "oficina": it can be a private room for one person (*despacho*).
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| The front desk and time clock **is** in reception. | The front desk and time clock **are** in the reception area. | Two things need `are`. |
| Each cubicle **have** a desk. | Each cubicle **has** a desk. | `Each` takes a singular verb. |
| We have the meeting in the **cafeteria room** with the whiteboard. | We have the meeting in the **conference room** with the whiteboard. | A cafeteria is for food, not for meetings. |
| Where is the **bathroom of washing hands**? | Where is the **washroom**? | Use the single word `washroom`. |
| The conference room has a **projector LCD**. | The conference room has an **LCD projector**. | In English, the adjective-like word comes first. |

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

Use: `cubicle`, `conference room`, `cafeteria`, `reception area`, `washroom`.

1. Visitors wait in the ______ before the meeting.
2. I work in a small ______ with a desk and a computer.
3. The team meets in the ______ to see the presentation.
4. People buy lunch in the ______.
5. Where is the ______? I would like to wash my hands.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence with the verb in brackets.

1. A desk, a chair, and a computer are in each cubicle. (have)
2. A whiteboard is in the conference room. (there is)
3. The front desk is in the reception area. (be located)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Give a short tour of your office (or home office). Name **four places**.
- Describe the **conference room** where you have meetings. What equipment does it have?
- A new colleague arrives today. Explain where the reception, the cafeteria and the washroom are.

## 8. Quick recap

- Reception area: front desk and time clock.
- Cubicle: desk, chair, computer. Conference room: whiteboard and LCD projector.
- Cafeteria: food. Washroom: wash hands and use the toilet.
- `Office` can mean the whole workplace or a room bigger than a cubicle.
- `Each` takes a singular verb: each cubicle **has**.
