# How to agree and disagree in a meeting

## 1. Learning objective

Show **complete agreement, polite disagreement and partial agreement** in a meeting, and use modals to suggest ideas and talk about possible results.

## 2. Key concepts

In a meeting, you often need to say if you accept or reject an idea. Choose your phrase by how **strong** your opinion is and how **polite** you want to be.

| Level | Phrases |
|-------|---------|
| Complete agreement | I fully agree with/that… · I'm in complete agreement with/that… |
| Complete disagreement | I absolutely disagree with/that… · I'm in total disagreement with/that… |
| Polite disagreement | I'm afraid I can't agree with/that… · I'm sorry, I disagree with/that… · I have to say, I disagree with/that… |
| Partial agreement | I would agree with you, but… · You may be right, but… |

:::tip With or that?
After `agree with`, use a **noun or pronoun**: *I fully agree with Sara.* After `agree that`, use a **full clause**: *I fully agree that we need more testing.*
:::

:::note Adverbs and adjectives add strength
`fully`, `absolutely`, `complete` and `total` make your opinion strong. Use them when you are sure. Do not use them to disagree with your manager in front of the whole team without a polite opening.
:::

### Modals to suggest ideas and talk about results

| Modal | Use | Example |
|-------|-----|---------|
| `can` | possibility | We can return the extra money after the project is completed. |
| `should` | advice, a good idea | We should try to negotiate for a higher price. |
| `might` | a possible result | If we set the price too high, our prospects might back out. |
| `could` | a possible problem | Her tardiness could become an issue. |

:::warning Partial agreement is not a yes
`I would agree with you, but…` and `You may be right, but…` mean you are **not sure**. The important part comes after `but`.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| I am agree with you. | I **agree** with you. | `agree` is a verb. Do not use `be` with it. |
| I am not agree with that. | I **disagree** with that. / I don't agree with that. | Use `disagree` or `don't agree`. |
| I'm in complete agreement that the plan. | I'm in complete agreement **with** the plan. | Use `with` + noun, or `that` + clause. |
| No, you are wrong. | I'm afraid I can't agree with that. | Direct words sound rude in a meeting. |
| She could becomes an issue. | Her tardiness could **become** an issue. | Modal + base verb, without `-s`. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
Do not say *I am agree* or *I am in agree*. In Spanish we say *estoy de acuerdo*, but in English `agree` is a verb: **I agree**.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. I ______ agree with you. We need more tests. (strong agreement)
2. I'm ______ I can't agree with that plan. (polite)
3. I would agree with you, ______ the budget is too small.
4. If we set the price too high, our prospects ______ back out. (possible result)

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Make each sentence more polite.

1. I disagree with your idea.
2. No, that is wrong.

### C. Speaking prompts

- Disagree politely with a colleague who wants to skip testing.
- Give partial agreement to a new deadline. Explain your concern after `but`.
- Suggest an idea with `should` and a possible result with `might`.

## 8. Quick recap

- Use `fully`, `absolutely`, `complete` and `total` for strong opinions.
- Use `I'm afraid…`, `I'm sorry…` or `I have to say…` to disagree politely.
- Use `I would agree with you, but…` for partial agreement.
- `agree with` + noun, `agree that` + clause. Never `I am agree`.
- Use `can`, `should`, `might` and `could` to suggest ideas and possible results.
