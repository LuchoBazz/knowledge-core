# How to communicate mindfully at work

## 1. Learning objective

Use expressions to communicate **mindfully** with co-workers: check how they feel, offer help, ask for and share opinions, respond kindly, and show appreciation.

## 2. Key concepts

Communicating mindfully means you listen, choose your words with care and think about how the other person feels. There are six useful functions.

| Function | Example expressions |
|----------|---------------------|
| Checking how someone feels | How are you today? · How is your day going so far? |
| Offering help | How can I support you? · I would be happy to … |
| Asking for opinions | I would appreciate your input on … · What are your thoughts on …? |
| Sharing opinions | What if we …? · Perhaps we could … |
| Responding to opinions | Thank you for being honest! · That is a valid point, but … |
| Showing appreciation | Thank you for … · I really appreciate … |

:::tip Check in with care
If a colleague seems different, ask gently: `You have not seemed like yourself lately. Is there something you want to talk about?` Do not force them to answer.
:::

:::note Offering help
Use a clear structure: `I understand you are … I am happy to help you with …` or `I know you've been dealing with … I wanted to offer to make myself available to …`.
:::

:::warning Soft words for opinions
Use `Perhaps we could …`, `What if we …?` and `I feel that …` instead of `You must …`. Soft language keeps the conversation open.
:::

### Responding to an opinion

- To accept: `Thank you for being honest!` · `This is just what we need.` · `That might be worth trying.`
- To think together: `Good point! Can we both think out loud for a minute?`
- To disagree kindly: `That is a valid point, but …` · `I think this needs a little/lot more thought.`
- To pause: `Shall we take a break and come back with a fresh perspective?`

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| You look bad today. | You have not seemed like yourself lately. Is there something you want to talk about? | Be gentle, not personal. |
| I am happy to help you in the report. | I am happy to help you **with** the report. | `help someone with something`. |
| I appreciate your help. (said to a bad idea) | I appreciate your input. That is a valid point, but … | Respond to the idea, then add your view. |
| I would appreciate you to send it. | I would appreciate **it if you could** send it. / I would appreciate **your** input. | `appreciate` is not followed by `you to`. |
| Thank you by your work. | Thank you **for** your work. | `thank someone for something`. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
In Spanish we say *gracias por tu ayuda* and *te ayudo en el informe*. In English, use `for` after `thank` and `with` after `help`: `Thank you for your help`, `I can help you with the report`.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. Thank you ______ your help with the demo.
2. I would ______ your input on the plan.
3. I am happy to help you ______ the report.
4. That is a valid point, ______ I think we need more thought.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence in a more mindful way.

1. What is wrong with you today?
2. Your idea is bad.

### C. Speaking prompts

- Check in with a colleague who looks tired. Offer help.
- Ask a teammate for their opinion and respond with `That is a valid point, but …`.
- Thank a colleague for something specific.

## 8. Quick recap

- Check in gently: `How are you doing?`, `How is your day going so far?`
- Offer help: `How can I support you?`, `I would be happy to …`
- Ask and share opinions with soft language: `What if we …?`, `Perhaps we could …`
- Respond with respect: `That is a valid point, but …`
- Show appreciation: `Thank you for …`, `I really appreciate …`

## 9. Review checklist

- [ ] I can check how a colleague feels without being intrusive.
- [ ] I can offer help in two different ways.
- [ ] I can ask for and share opinions politely.
- [ ] I can respond to an idea with respect.
- [ ] I use `thank you for` and `help with` correctly.
