# How to conduct a project review meeting

## 1. Learning objective

Lead a project review meeting in five steps: **set the tone, review the project, review the outcome, ask for feedback, and wrap up**.

## 2. Key concepts

A project review meeting looks back at a finished project. The goal is to learn what worked, what did not, and what to improve next time.

| Step | Goal | Example phrases |
|------|------|-----------------|
| 1. Setting the tone | Thank people, explain the purpose | Thank you for attending this project review. · The purpose of this meeting is … |
| 2. Reviewing the project | Talk about methods and roles | What did we do well? · Which parts were difficult? |
| 3. Reviewing the outcome | Check results | What did the team accomplish? · Did they make an impact? |
| 4. Asking for feedback and recommendations | Collect ideas | What can we do better next time? |
| 5. Wrapping up | Summarize and thank | To recap, … · Thank you for your honest feedback. |

:::tip Start positive
Begin with thanks: `I appreciate all your efforts in making this project successful.` People share more honest feedback when they feel valued.
:::

:::note Use the past simple for finished work
Most review questions use the **past simple** because the project is finished: `What did we do well?`, `What did the team accomplish?`
:::

:::warning Talk about the work, not the person
Say `Which parts of our methods were difficult?` instead of `Who made mistakes?`. A review is not a trial.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| What we did well? | What **did we do** well? | Questions need an auxiliary before the subject. |
| What do the team accomplish? | What **did** the team accomplish? | The project is finished, so use the past simple. |
| Who made mistakes? | Which parts of our methods were difficult? | Focus on the process, not on blaming people. |
| Resume, we agreed to test more. | **To recap**, we agreed to test more. | `resume` is a false friend. Use `recap` or `summarize`. |
| I assure you that your feedback is noted duly. | I assure you that your feedback is **duly noted**. | `duly noted` is a fixed expression. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
*Resumir* is not `resume`. In English, `resume` means *continue* (*We will resume the meeting after lunch*). To summarize, say `to recap` or `to summarize`.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. Thank you ______ attending this project review.
2. The ______ of this meeting is to learn from the project.
3. What ______ the team accomplish?
4. To ______, we agreed to share weekly updates.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence to be more constructive.

1. Who caused the delay?
2. Was the project bad?

### C. Speaking prompts

- Open a project review with a thank-you and the purpose of the meeting (30 seconds).
- Ask three questions: one about methods, one about the outcome and one about the next time.
- Close the meeting with `To recap, …` and a thank-you.

## 8. Quick recap

- A project review has five steps: tone, project, outcome, feedback, wrap-up.
- Start with thanks and the purpose of the meeting.
- Use the past simple for finished work.
- Ask about the process, not about people.
- End with `To recap, …` and a thank-you.
