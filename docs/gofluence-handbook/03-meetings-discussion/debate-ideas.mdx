# How to debate ideas III

:::info Assumptions
This is lesson III of a series. We assume you already know basic ways to agree and disagree. See [How to agree and disagree in a meeting](./agree-and-disagree-in-a-meeting.mdx) first if you need a review.
:::

## 1. Learning objective

Express opinions **assertively** in a debate with gerunds and modals, and make **counterarguments** with conjunctions and adverbs.

## 2. Key concepts

An assertive opinion is clear and confident, but still respectful. Three tools help you.

### Gerunds to state an opinion

After verbs like `support` and expressions like `put our vote behind`, use the **-ing form**.

- I/We support **having** a paperless office.
- I/We are putting our vote behind **exploring** business opportunities in China.

### Modals to make arguments

| Phrase | Example |
|--------|---------|
| The way I see it, we should ... | The way I see it, we **should** charge the client for shipping. |
| I am convinced that we must ... | I am convinced that we **must** expand our marketing efforts to digital media. |
| If you look at it from my perspective, you'll see why we have to ... | If you look at it from my perspective, you'll see why we **have to** revisit the contract. |

### Conjunctions and adverbs for objections

| Phrase | Use |
|--------|-----|
| But don't you think…? | A soft challenge as a question |
| I hate to be the one to point out that…, but… | A careful objection |
| You have a point. However,… | Accept, then object |
| You could say that, but/although… | Partly accept |
| That may be true, but/although… | Partly accept |

:::tip Accept first, then object
`You have a point. However,…` shows respect before you disagree. People listen more when they feel heard.
:::

:::warning Strong modals
`must` and `have to` are very strong. Use them when you are sure and have a reason. For a softer opinion, use `should`.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| I support to have a paperless office. | I support **having** a paperless office. | `support` is followed by the -ing form. |
| I am convinced that we must to expand. | I am convinced that we **must expand**. | Modal + base verb, without `to`. |
| That may be true, but however, ... | That may be true, **but** ... | Do not use `but` and `however` together. |
| You have a point, however we need more time. | You have a point**. However,** we need more time. | `however` starts a new sentence, with a comma after it. |
| I hate to say but... | I hate to **be the one to point out that…, but**… | Use the complete, polite structure. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
In Spanish we often say *apoyo a tener* or *apoyo tener*. In English, after `support` use **-ing**: `I support having…`. Also, English does not use `to` after a modal: `must expand`, not `must to expand`.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. We support ______ (use) open-source tools.
2. I am convinced that we ______ revisit the plan. (strong modal)
3. You have a point. ______, the cost is too high.
4. That may be true, ______ we do not have time.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence to sound assertive and polite.

1. No. You are wrong about the deadline.
2. We want to explore the market in Brazil. (use `putting our vote behind`)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Defend an idea (for example, working from home) in 30 seconds. Use one gerund phrase and one modal.
- Answer a colleague's argument with `You have a point. However,…`.
- Raise a concern with `I hate to be the one to point out that…`.

## 8. Quick recap

- Use the -ing form after `support` and `putting our vote behind`.
- Use `should`, `must` and `have to` to make arguments.
- Accept first, then object: `You have a point. However,…`
- `But don't you think…?` is a soft challenge.
- Do not mix `but` and `however` in the same place.

## 9. Review checklist

- [ ] I know three ways to raise an objection politely.
- [ ] I can accept a point and then disagree.
- [ ] I can defend an idea for 30 seconds.
