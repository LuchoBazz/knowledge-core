# Making suggestions

:::info Assumptions
This lesson joins two parts of the class notes: general phrases for making suggestions, and phrases for discussing business options with conditionals.
:::

## 1. Learning objective

Make suggestions with **modals and question words**, and discuss business options using **conditionals** and phrases like `What if…` and `An alternative…`.

## 2. Key concepts

### Phrases to make suggestions

- I/We suggest ...
- Let's ...

Modals and question words are also useful:

| Structure | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| You/We/He/She/It/They **could** ... | We could move the meeting to Monday. |
| You/We/He/She/They **might (not) want to** ... | You might want to check the numbers again. |
| **What/How about** ... ? | How about using a shared document? |
| **Why don't/doesn't** you/we/they/he/she/it ... ? | Why don't we ask the client? |
| **Why not** ... ? | Why not test it first? |

:::tip Softer or stronger?
`Let's…` and `I suggest…` are direct. `You might want to…` and `We could…` are softer. Use soft forms with your manager or a client.
:::

:::warning Verb forms after each phrase
- `I suggest` + **-ing** or **that** clause: *I suggest meeting early.*
- `Let's` + **base verb**: *Let's start now.*
- `could`, `might want to` + **base verb**.
- `What/How about` + **-ing**: *How about using a template?*
- `Why don't we` + **base verb**: *Why don't we ask?*
:::

### Discussing business options

Use the **first conditional** to compare options and results.

- If we implement a workforce reduction plan, our company **will be able to** cope with the global recession.
- If we freeze hiring employees, we **won't have to** fire some of our best employees.
- If we do this, then the company **will lose** a great deal of talent.

Other helpful phrases:

| Phrase | Example |
|--------|---------|
| What if ... | What if we consider outsourcing overseas? |
| An alternative ... | An alternative could be stopping all salary increases and bonuses. |
| How about ... | How about we implement cost-cutting strategies? |

:::note If + present, will + base verb
In a conditional, use the **present simple** after `if` and `will` in the result. Do not use `will` after `if`. See the [first conditional](../01-grammar/first-conditional.mdx).
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| I suggest to move the meeting. | I suggest **moving** the meeting. | `suggest` is followed by -ing or a `that` clause, not `to`. |
| Let's to start now. | Let's **start** now. | `Let's` + base verb. |
| Why we don't ask the client? | Why **don't we** ask the client? | Question order: auxiliary before the subject. |
| How about to use a template? | How about **using** a template? | `How about` + -ing. |
| If we will freeze hiring, we won't fire anyone. | If we **freeze** hiring, we won't fire anyone. | No `will` after `if`. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
In Spanish we say *sugiero mover* or *sugiero que movamos*. In English, **do not** say *I suggest to move*. Use `I suggest moving` or `I suggest that we move`.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. I suggest ______ (hire) one more developer.
2. Why ______ we ask the vendor for a discount?
3. How about ______ (use) a shared spreadsheet?
4. If we freeze hiring, we ______ (not / have to) fire anyone.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence with the phrase in brackets.

1. We should test the product first. (Why not)
2. Stop all bonuses. (An alternative could be)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Suggest three ways to reduce costs at your company. Use `What if…`, `How about…` and `An alternative…`.
- Make a soft suggestion to your manager with `You might want to…`.
- Compare two options with the first conditional.

## 8. Quick recap

- Direct: `I suggest…`, `Let's…`. Softer: `We could…`, `You might want to…`.
- `suggest` and `How about` + -ing. `Let's` and `Why don't we` + base verb.
- Discuss options with `What if…`, `An alternative…` and `How about…`.
- Use first conditional: if + present, will + base verb.
