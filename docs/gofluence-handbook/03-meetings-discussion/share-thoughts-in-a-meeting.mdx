# How to share thoughts in a meeting

## 1. Learning objective

Ask for and express opinions in a meeting using **verb structures** (`think`, `feel`, `believe`) and **noun structures** (`opinion`, `thoughts`, `views`).

## 2. Key concepts

There are two main ways to ask for an opinion and to give one: with **verbs** or with **nouns**.

| | Asking for an opinion | Giving an opinion |
|---|---|---|
| Verbs | What do you think of/about ... ? | I think/feel/believe (that) ... |
| | What can you say about ... ? | |
| | How do you feel about ... ? | |
| Nouns | What's your opinion of/about/on ... ? | In my opinion, ... |
| | What are your thoughts/views/comments on/about ... ? | |
| | I'd like to know your opinion/thoughts on/about ... | |
| | I'd like to hear your comments on/about ... | |

:::tip Pick the right preposition
Many of these phrases accept two prepositions: `think of/about`, `opinion of/about/on`, `thoughts on/about`. Both are correct. Choose one and stay consistent.
:::

:::note Direct or indirect?
- **Direct question:** What do you think of the new design?
- **Indirect, more formal:** I'd like to know your opinion on the new design.

Use the indirect form with senior people or in formal meetings.
:::

:::warning Verb or noun after the preposition
After `of`, `about` and `on`, use a **noun or an -ing form**: *What do you think about changing the schedule?* Do not use the base verb.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| What do you think about to change the plan? | What do you think about **changing** the plan? | After a preposition, use the -ing form. |
| What is your opinion in the plan? | What is your opinion **of/about/on** the plan? | `opinion` takes `of`, `about` or `on`, not `in`. |
| In my opinion, I think we need more time. | **In my opinion**, we need more time. / **I think** we need more time. | Do not repeat the opinion marker. |
| What are your thought on this? | What are your **thoughts** on this? | `thoughts` is plural after `your` in this question. |
| I feel that the plan is bad, and you? | I feel that the plan is bad. **What do you think?** | Use a full question. It sounds more professional. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
In Spanish we say *En mi opinión, yo creo que…*. In English, use **one** marker only: `In my opinion, …` or `I think …`. Also, the question is *What do you think **of/about**…?*, not *What do you think **to**…?*
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. What do you ______ of the new logo?
2. How do you ______ about the deadline?
3. What's your opinion ______ the new tool?
4. In my ______, we need a clearer plan.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Make each sentence more formal with `I'd like to...`.

1. What do you think of the proposal?
2. Tell me your comments on the report.

### C. Speaking prompts

- Ask two colleagues for their opinion on a new process. Use one verb phrase and one noun phrase.
- Give your opinion about remote work. Use `I think that…` and `In my opinion, …`.
- Ask your manager politely for comments on your draft.

## 8. Quick recap

- Verbs: `think`, `feel`, `believe`, `say`.
- Nouns: `opinion`, `thoughts`, `views`, `comments`.
- Use an -ing form after `of`, `about` and `on`.
- Use `I'd like to know/hear ...` for a more formal request.
- Use only one opinion marker in a sentence.

## 9. Review checklist
