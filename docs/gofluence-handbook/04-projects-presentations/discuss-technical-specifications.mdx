# Discuss technical specifications

:::info Assumptions
The class notes list four key phrases only. This page builds simple practice around them: reviewing a part, adjusting a measurement, suggesting a material and checking feasibility. The examples use product and engineering design, but the phrases also work for software requirements.
:::

## 1. Learning objective

Review, adjust and question technical specifications using four key phrases.

## 2. Key concepts

Technical specifications (or **specs**) describe how a product or system must be built. In meetings, you often **review**, **adjust**, **suggest changes** and **check if something is possible**.

| Purpose | Key phrase |
|---------|-----------|
| Review | Let's review the ... of the ... |
| Adjust | Can we adjust the ... by [measurement]? |
| Suggest | I suggest we switch to [material], considering its [property]. |
| Check feasibility | Is this design feasible within our ...? |

```text title="Phrases with examples"
Let's review the dimensions of the casing.
Can we adjust the width by 2 millimeters?
I suggest we switch to aluminum, considering its low weight.
Is this design feasible within our budget?
```

:::tip Be specific
Always add a number, a unit or a property. `Can we adjust the width by 2 mm?` is clearer than `Can we change the size?`.
:::

:::note Grammar of the key phrases
- `Let's` plus base verb: `Let's review`.
- `adjust ... by` plus measurement: the change amount.
- `I suggest we` plus base verb: `I suggest we switch`.
- `considering` plus noun: the reason.
- `feasible within` plus noun: the limit (budget, time, size).
:::

:::warning `feasible` is not the same as `possible`
`Feasible` means *viable, posible de hacer en la práctica*. Something can be possible but not feasible with your budget.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Let's to review the specs. | Let's **review** the specs. | `Let's` is followed by the base verb. |
| Can we adjust the width **of** 2 mm? | Can we adjust the width **by** 2 mm? | Use `by` for the amount of change. |
| I suggest we **to switch** to steel. | I suggest we **switch** to steel. | No `to` after `suggest we`. |
| I suggest to switch to steel. | I suggest **switching** to steel. | After `suggest`, use `-ing` or `(that) we` plus base verb. |
| Is this design feasible **in** our budget? | Is this design feasible **within** our budget? | `Within` shows the limit. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
Do not say `I suggest you to do`. In English, `suggest` does not take `to` plus verb. Say `I suggest that you do`, `I suggest you do` or `I suggest doing`.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. ______ review the dimensions of the casing.
2. Can we adjust the width ______ 2 millimeters?
3. I suggest we ______ to aluminum, considering its low weight.
4. Is this design feasible ______ our budget?

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence with the key phrase in brackets.

1. The material should be lighter. (I suggest we switch to ..., considering ...)
2. Check the size of the screen. (Let's review ...)
3. Can the team finish this by June? (Is this design feasible within ...?)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Role-play a design review: review one part, ask for an adjustment and suggest a material.
- Ask whether a feature is feasible within a deadline.

## 8. Quick recap

- `Let's review the ... of the ...` opens a spec discussion.
- `Can we adjust the ... by ...?` asks for a change in a measurement.
- `I suggest we switch to ..., considering its ...` gives a reason.
- `Is this design feasible within our ...?` checks limits.
- Be specific: add numbers, units and properties.

## 9. Review checklist

- [ ] I can say the four key phrases from memory.
- [ ] I can adjust a measurement using `by`.
- [ ] I can suggest a material and give a reason.
- [ ] I can check feasibility within a budget or timeline.
- [ ] I avoid `suggest to` plus a verb.
