# Expressing concerns and risks

## 1. Learning objective

Introduce a worry, a possible problem or a risk clearly and politely, and name the consequence.

## 2. Key concepts

We use special phrases to show **worry or caution**. They introduce a possible problem or a negative result. They also show that you think critically and see possible dangers.

```text title="Structure"
Opening phrase + specific issue or consequence
```

| Opening phrase | Example |
|----------------|---------|
| One concern is that ... | One concern is that people might become too dependent on technology and lose basic skills. |
| The problem with ... is that ... | The problem with relying on AI is that it can sometimes make mistakes or provide biased results. |
| There is a risk that ... | There is a risk that climate change could lead to more extreme weather events. |

:::tip Use modals for possible results
A risk is something that is **possible**, not certain. Use `might`, `could` and `can`.

- There is a risk that the server **could** fail during the launch.
- The data **might** be wrong.
:::

:::note After each phrase, give the detail
Do not stop after `One concern is that ...`. Add the issue or the consequence. A concern with a clear consequence is easier to discuss and solve.
:::

:::warning Say `risk of` plus noun, `risk that` plus clause
- There is a risk **of** delay.
- There is a risk **that** we will miss the deadline.
:::

:::info Pronunciation
- take up one's time: /teɪk ʌp wʌnz taɪm/
- cut the time in half: /kʌt ðə taɪm ɪn hæf/
- get anxious: /ɡɛt ˈæŋkʃəs/ (the `x` sounds like `ksh`)
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| One concern is **what** people become dependent. | One concern is **that** people might become dependent. | Use `that` to introduce the clause. |
| There is a risk **to** fail. | There is a risk **of failing** or a risk **that** we fail. | Do not use `risk to` plus a verb. |
| The problem with AI **it** makes mistakes. | The problem with AI **is that it** makes mistakes. | The structure needs `is that`. |
| The meeting **took out** an hour of my time. | The meeting **took up** an hour of my time. | The phrasal verb is `take up`. |
| The new tool can cut the time **to half**. | The new tool can cut the time **in half**. | The fixed phrase is `in half`. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
Do not translate *me preocupa que* word by word. In meetings, `One concern is that ...` sounds more professional. Also, `anxious` means *nervioso* or *inquieto*. It is not the medical word *ansiedad*.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. One ______ is that people might lose basic skills.
2. The problem ______ relying on AI is that it can make mistakes.
3. There is a ______ that the server could fail.
4. The meeting took ______ an hour of my time.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Rewrite each sentence with the phrase in brackets.

1. People may depend too much on technology. (One concern is that ...)
2. AI sometimes gives biased results. (The problem with relying on AI is that ...)
3. The update might break the app. (There is a risk that ...)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Name one concern about a new tool in your team. Use `One concern is that ...`.
- Describe a time you got anxious at work. What happened?
- Explain how a tool can cut the time in half for a task.

## 8. Quick recap

- Use `One concern is that ...`, `The problem with ... is that ...` and `There is a risk that ...`.
- Always add the specific issue or consequence.
- Use `might`, `could` and `can` for possible results.
- `take up one's time`, `cut the time in half` and `get anxious` are useful everyday phrases.
- Say `risk of` plus noun or `-ing`, `risk that` plus clause.
