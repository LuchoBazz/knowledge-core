# Present a project proposal

## 1. Learning objective

Present a project proposal in a clear order: opening, outline, goals, problem, solution, phases, costs and conclusion.

## 2. Key concepts

A good proposal presentation follows a **fixed structure**. Your audience always knows where you are.

```text title="Proposal structure"
1. Begin the presentation
2. Give an outline
3. State the main idea and goals
4. State the present situation and problems
5. Introduce the solution and actions
6. Move from one part to another
7. Explain stages, phases and deadlines
8. Show the estimated costs (visual aids)
9. Conclude
```

| Step | Useful sentence |
|------|-----------------|
| Begin | Today, I will present our project proposal for Company A's Static Printer. |
| Outline | First, I will explain the concept. Then, I will talk about the timeline. After that, I will show you our costings. |
| Goals | The project aims to lower energy usage by using static electricity. |
| Problem | At the moment, regular inkjet printers use electrical pulses to transfer ink. |
| Solution | Because of this, we started brainstorming to create an initial design. |
| Move on | For the next part of my presentation, please refer to the table on the screen. |
| Phases | Phase 1 will involve building our initial design. |
| Costs | As you can see from this chart, the estimated cost is 2 million euros. |
| Conclude | In summary, I would like to emphasize how helpful our project can be. |

:::tip Use infinitives to sound professional
Infinitives are short and clear when you open, state the aim or change topic.

- **To start** this presentation ...
- The aim of this project **is to reduce** the operation costs ...
- I would like **to move on** to my next topic ...
:::

:::note Phases use `will`
Phases and deadlines are plans for the future. Use `will involve`, `will include` and `will be about`.
:::

:::warning Say one idea per sentence
In a live presentation, long sentences are hard to follow. Pause after each step of your outline.
:::

## 5. Common mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Today I present you our proposal. | Today, I **will present** our proposal. | Use `will` for what you are about to do in the presentation. |
| At the moment, regular printers **uses** pulses. | At the moment, regular printers **use** pulses. | Plural subject needs the base verb. |
| The estimated cost is **of** 2 million euros. | The estimated cost is 2 million euros. | No `of` after `is` here. |
| Phase 1 will involves building the design. | Phase 1 will **involve** building the design. | Use the base verb after `will`. |
| First I will explain the concept, then I show the costs. | First, I will explain the concept. Then, I **will show** the costs. | Keep the same future form in the whole outline. |

:::danger Spanish speakers
Do not translate *presentar* as `present` in every case. To introduce a person, say `introduce`. Use `present` for a proposal, report or idea.
:::

## 6. Practice exercises

### A. Fill in the blanks

1. ______ start this presentation, I would like to thank you for coming.
2. First, I ______ (explain) the concept. Then, I ______ (talk) about the budget.
3. Phase 2 will ______ (include) installing the new server.
4. As you can ______ from this chart, the cost is 50,000 dollars.

### B. Rewrite the sentence

Make each sentence more suitable for a formal presentation.

1. We want to cut costs.  (Start with: The aim of this project ...)
2. Now look at the table.  (Start with: For the next part ...)

### C. Speaking prompts

- Give a 1-minute outline for a proposal about a new internal tool.
- Explain three phases of a project and one deadline for each.

## 8. Quick recap

- Follow a fixed order: begin, outline, goals, problem, solution, phases, costs, conclusion.
- Use `will` to announce the outline and the phases.
- Use `To start ...`, `The aim is to ...` and `I would like to move on to ...`.
- Refer to visual aids with `As you can see from this chart, ...`.
- End with `In summary, ...`.

## 9. Review checklist

- [ ] I can list the main steps of a proposal presentation.
- [ ] I can give a three-step outline with `First`, `Then` and `After that`.
- [ ] I can describe three phases with `will involve`, `will include` and `will be about`.
- [ ] I can introduce a chart or table.
- [ ] I can close the presentation with a clear summary.
