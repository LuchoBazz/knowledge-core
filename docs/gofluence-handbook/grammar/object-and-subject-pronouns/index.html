Object and subject pronouns

Assumptions

The notes list object pronouns (you, me, him/her, them) and subject pronouns (I, you, they). This page also adds the rest of the subject, object and possessive set so you see the full picture.

1. Learning objective​

Use subject pronouns before a verb and object pronouns after a verb or a preposition, so your English is clear and not repetitive.

2. Key concepts​

A pronoun replaces a noun. The form changes depending on its job in the sentence.

  • Subject pronoun: it does the action. It goes before the verb.
  • Object pronoun: it receives the action. It follows a verb or a preposition. It can be singular or plural.

ititits(rare)

Subject, verb, object
She (subject)  sent (verb)  them (object)  the report.
They (subject) called (verb) me (object).
Send the file to (preposition) him (object).
After a preposition, use an object pronoun
  • with me, for us, to them, about her, from him
  • Between you and me (not: between you and I).
It and you

it and you have the same form as subject and object. Only the position changes: You called me / I called you.

Possessives have no apostrophe

It is its, not it's (it is). Write yours, hers, theirs, not your's, her's or their's.

4. Examples in a workplace context​

  • Email
  • Stand-up
  • Code review
Email to a colleague
Hi Luis,

Maria sent me the design. I reviewed it, and she asked me to share it with you.
I will forward it to them (the client team) after you give me your comments.

Thanks,
Ana
Stand-up update
I talked to him about the bug, and he will fix it today.
We asked them for access, and they gave us a test account.
Pull request comment
Please check the PR. It is ours, and the tests are yours.
I explained the change to her, and she approved it.
💬 Team chat

Ben: Did you send the invoice to the client?

Eva: Yes, I sent it to them this morning.

Ben: Did they answer you?

Eva: Not yet. I will call them if they do not reply today.

5. Common mistakes​

❌ Wrong✅ CorrectWhy
Me and Sara will present.Sara and I will present.I is the subject. Put the other person first.
She gave the report to I.She gave the report to me.Object pronoun after a preposition.
Send the files to they.Send the files to them.them is the object form of they.
Him is the project lead.He is the project lead.He is the subject form.
The company changed it's policy.The company changed its policy.its (possessive) has no apostrophe.
Spanish speakers

In Spanish you can drop the subject (Envío el informe). In English you must say it: I send the report. Also, English has no le / les in front of the verb. Say I told him and I sent them the file.

6. Practice exercises​

A. Fill in the blanks​

Choose the correct pronoun.

  1. Tom is new. Can you help ______ (he / him) with the setup?
  2. ______ (We / Us) finished the report before the deadline.
  3. The clients are waiting. Please send ______ (they / them) the proposal.
  4. This is not my laptop. It is ______ (her / hers).

B. Rewrite the sentence​

Replace the words in bold with a pronoun.

  1. Maria and Pedro are in the meeting. I will call Maria and Pedro later.
  2. The manager asked my team and me for an update.

C. Speaking prompts​

  • Describe your team to a new colleague. Use he, she, they, him, her and them.
  • Tell a short story about a request from a client. Use at least three object pronouns.

D. Quick quiz​

Choose the correct sentence.

7. Answer key​

Show answers

A.

  1. him
  2. We
  3. them
  4. hers

B.

  1. They are in the meeting. I will call them later.
  2. The manager asked us for an update.

C. Answers vary. Check that subject pronouns come before the verb and object pronouns follow a verb or a preposition.

8. Quick recap​

  • Subject pronouns come before the verb: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.
  • Object pronouns come after a verb or a preposition: me, you, him, her, it, us, them.
  • Possessives show ownership: my / mine, your / yours, its, our / ours, their / theirs.
  • Always say the subject in English.

9. Review checklist​

  • I can list subject and object pronouns from memory.
  • I use an object pronoun after a preposition.
  • I say Sara and I, not me and Sara, as a subject.
  • I do not write it's for the possessive.
  • I can use pronouns to avoid repeating names in an email.
