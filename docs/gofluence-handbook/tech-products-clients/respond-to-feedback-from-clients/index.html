How to respond to feedback from clients

1. Learning objective​

Respond to negative feedback from a client in a calm, professional way. Apologize, show empathy, explain the solution and keep a friendly tone.

2. Key concepts​

A good response has several steps. You do not need all of them every time, but the order helps.

  1. Apologize.
  2. Clarify the problem.
  3. Show empathy.
  4. Take responsibility.
  5. Explain how and when you will solve it.
  6. Offer an alternative.
  7. Summarize.
  8. Promise a follow-up.
  9. End with a friendly tone.

The phrases, step by step​

StepUseful phrases
ApologizeFirst of all, I'd like to say how sorry I am. / I'm so (very, really, terribly) sorry about that. / I apologize for ... / Let me apologize for ...
ClarifyIf I understand you correctly, ... / Could you please tell me exactly what happened? / Could you explain ... a little more? / Would you mind if I just check ... again?
Show empathyI can see why ... / I understand. / I see what you mean. / I would feel the same way. / What a difficult situation this puts you in.
Take responsibilityIt looks like an oversight on our part. / There seems to be a misunderstanding. / It appears ... got overlooked. / I'm afraid there has been some sort of mix-up.
Explain the solutionI'll take care of this at once for you. / I'm sure we can find a solution. / This should be resolved by the end of ... / You'll receive ... by ...
Offer an alternativeMay I offer ... as an alternative for ...? / I'll look into other possible ways of ... / Perhaps you could consider ... as another option.
SummarizeWhat we have decided is ... / Our action plan is ... / I'd like to review this once more to make sure we agree.
Follow upI'll get back to you ... / I'll make sure that ...
End friendlyThanks for bringing this to our attention. / Is there anything else I can help you with today?
Clarify before you promise

Repeat the problem in your own words: If I understand you correctly, the invoice shows the wrong amount. This shows you are listening.

Softening responsibility

Phrases like There seems to be a misunderstanding and It looks like an oversight are soft and polite. Use them to accept the issue without blaming a person.

Apologize with for

Use apologize for + noun or -ing: I apologize for the delay. / I apologize for sending the wrong file.

4. Examples in a workplace context​

💬 Phone call with an unhappy client

Client: I paid for the premium plan, but my account still shows the free plan.

Agent: First of all, I'm terribly sorry about that. If I understand you correctly, your payment worked but the plan did not change?

Client: Exactly. This is the second time.

Agent: I can see why you are upset. It looks like an oversight on our part. I'll take care of this at once for you.

Client: How long will it take?

Agent: This should be resolved by the end of today. I'll get back to you this afternoon.

  • Call summary
Reply to a client complaint
Subject: RE: Wrong invoice

Dear Ms. Rivera,

Thank you for bringing this to our attention. Let me apologize for the mix-up with your invoice.
It looks like an oversight on our part.
You'll receive a corrected invoice by tomorrow at noon.
I'd be glad to offer a 10 percent discount to make up for this inconvenience.

Please reach out again should you have any more concerns.

Kind regards,
Andrés
End of a call
What we have decided is this: we will send a corrected file today.
Our action plan is to review your account and confirm the change by Friday.
I'd like to review this once more to make sure we agree.
I'll make sure that you receive a confirmation email.
Is there anything else I can help you with today?

5. Common mistakes​

❌ Wrong✅ CorrectWhy
I apologize by the delay.I apologize for the delay.apologize for.
Can you explain me what happened?Could you tell me exactly what happened? / Could you explain to me...explain needs to before a person.
It was your fault.There seems to be a misunderstanding.Avoid blame. Use a polite, neutral phrase.
I will resolve at once your problem.I'll take care of this at once for you. / I'll resolve your problem at once.Do not put at once between the verb and its object.
I'll make sure that you will receive it.I'll make sure that you receive it.Use the present simple after make sure that for a future result.
Spanish speakers

Do not write I am sorry for the inconveniences just because inconvenientes is plural in Spanish. Use the singular: the inconvenience. Also avoid It is your fault (es su culpa). It sounds rude in English.

6. Practice exercises​

A. Fill in the blanks​

  1. First of all, I'm terribly ______ about that.
  2. If I ______ you correctly, the file did not arrive.
  3. It looks like an ______ on our part.
  4. I'll get ______ to you by Friday.

B. Rewrite the sentence​

Make each sentence more polite and professional.

  1. It is your mistake.
  2. Tell me what happened.

C. Speaking prompts​

  • Role-play an unhappy client call. Use at least one phrase from each of these steps: apologize, empathy, solution and follow-up.
  • A client says, "Your app lost my data!" What do you say first?

D. Quick quiz​

Which phrase shows empathy?

7. Answer key​

Show answers

A.

  1. sorry
  2. understand
  3. oversight
  4. back

B.

  1. There seems to be a misunderstanding. (or It looks like an oversight on our part.)
  2. Could you please tell me exactly what happened?

C. Answers vary. Check that you apologize first, repeat the problem, show empathy, say when you will fix it, and promise a follow-up.

8. Quick recap​

  • Start with an apology: I apologize for ..., I'm terribly sorry about that.
  • Clarify and show empathy: If I understand you correctly, ..., I can see why ....
  • Take responsibility softly: It looks like an oversight on our part.
  • Say how and when: This should be resolved by ..., I'll get back to you ....
  • End friendly: Is there anything else I can help you with today?

9. Review checklist​

  • I can apologize with three different phrases.
  • I can repeat the client's problem with If I understand you correctly.
  • I can show empathy without blaming anyone.
  • I can explain how and when I will fix a problem.
  • I can offer an alternative and promise a follow-up.
  • I can end the conversation in a friendly way.
